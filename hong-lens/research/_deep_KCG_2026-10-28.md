## 1. One-page answer

**Verdict: enter small (3.75–6.25% of portfolio), not the full 7.5% slot — growth is real on the numbers but half of it is a cost cycle, and the balance sheet fails Hong gate 2 on cash.** Confidence: **medium-high** on the figures (all from SET/SETSMART/company), **medium** on the growth-source reading (segment cost data not disclosed numerically), **low** on anything sourced only from panphol Opp Day summaries (see §10).

Why "growth is real":
1. **Net profit has risen three straight years at Hong's ~26% pace**: 2023 +26.9%, 2024 +32.7%, 2025 +24.0% (SN FY2025). 3-year NP CAGR 2022→2025 = **27.8%** (241.1 → 503.3 m). Shares have been fixed at 545 m since the Aug 2023 IPO, so for a buyer today NP growth = per-share growth. The "17%" EPS CAGR quoted in `hong-keep-or-cut-2026-10-28.md:68` uses SET's 2023 EPS 0.67, which is on a pre-IPO weighted share count — understated (see §5).
2. **2026 is still growing at that pace**: H1/26 NP 276.8 m **+25.2%** y-y, Q2/26 122.2 m **+23.5%** (CN 14 Aug 2026; matches SS). TTM NP (Q3/25–Q2/26) = 559.0 m, **+21.3%** vs the prior four quarters; new all-time high.
3. **Profit is cash**: CFO 2025 = 1,113.8 m vs NP 503.3 → 2.2x; H1/26 CFO 580.0 m vs NP 276.8 → 2.1x (SS/SN). Interest-bearing debt has been cut from 1,826 m (2023) to 947 m (end 2025) to 508 m (Q1/26) (SF).

Why only "small":
4. **Sales growth has stalled while profit grows**: revenue 2023→2025 grew 12%/yr, but H1/26 sales **+4.0%** and Q2/26 **+0.3%** (CN). Q2 profit +23.5% on flat sales = margin, and the company says the margin came from "lower raw material costs" — butter and cheese. European butter fell from a record €8,375/t (Nov 2024) to €3,835/t (Feb 2026), lowest since 2021, -35% y-y in Apr 2026 (dairynews.today / vespertool / expana, see §4). KCG is buying at the bottom of the dairy-fat cycle. `guess:` 2026 GM 31% is cycle-high, not structural — same shape as TOA in 2025.
5. **Hong gate 2 (cash covers short-term debt + dividend) fails hard**: cash 88.8 m vs bank OD 508.4 m at 31 Mar 2026 (0.17x); vs OD + FY2025 dividend (278 m) = 0.11x (SF). The May 2026 stage-2 review cut KCG for exactly this (`_master_hong-lens_2026-05-21.md:72`). Mitigant: CFO 1,114 m/yr covers OD + dividend 0.9x, and the OD is working-capital funding for an importer (inventories 1,423 m, receivables 1,912 m at end 2025). It is a business that runs on bank lines, not on cash.
6. **Liquidity of the stock is thin**: free float 27.43%, value traded 8.75 m baht/day YTD (SF 20/05/2026); Kim Chua Group holds 52.18%. A 6.25% slot (~ hundreds of thousands baht) is fine; getting out in a hurry is not.
7. **Forward growth the company itself targets is 10–15%/yr, not 26%** (JUMP+ plan, March 2026: sales +6–8%/yr, NP +10–15%/yr, NP 2028 ≈ 750–800 m — SN 3M/2026). Brokers forecast 2026 NP 549–554 m (+9–11%) (TR 15 May 2026; Yuanta via hoonvision 14 May 2026). H1 already delivered 277 m, so those forecasts imply an H2 decline — `guess:` they are stale and will be raised; our base case is ~587 m (+17%).

**What Art and the head must decide (not orders):**
- A. Which slot: the 3.75% (MOSHI-size) or 6.25% (SECURE-size) weight. §9 says the 7.5% slot is too big for a stock with 8.75 m/day turnover and a failed cash gate.
- B. Accept a Hong gate-2 fail for an importer whose working capital is bank-funded? The May 2026 stage-2 said no. Nothing in the balance sheet has changed except the OD is smaller (947 → 508 m) and CFO is larger.
- C. Entry timing vs the cost cycle: butter at a 5-year low is in the current margin. Q3/26 (≈ 12–13 Nov 2026) is the first quarter where GDT butter stopped falling (4,740 USD/t on 6 Oct 2026, roughly flat — commodity-board.com). If Art wants to see one quarter of margin behaviour with flat input costs, wait; if Art is buying the 3-year record and the 5% yield, the data supports it now at PE 10.1.
- D. Hong's 30:30:30:10 entry rule (`_master_hong-lens_2026-05-21.md:52`) fits this stock: first 30% now, rest on pullbacks / after Q3.

## 4. The growth story (the heart of this leg)

**Where the 2022 → 2025 profit growth came from (NP +262 m, 241 → 503):**
- Revenue +2,489 m (6,157 → 8,645, +12.0%/yr). At the 2022 net margin of 3.9% that alone adds only ~97 m.
- Margin expansion adds the other ~165 m: GM 28.7 → 30.9% (+2.2 pt = +190 m gross profit on 2025 sales); SG&A/sales 24.0 → 23.5% (+43 m); finance cost 80 → 43 m (IPO proceeds repaid debt, rates down); tax roughly proportional.
- → **Roughly 37% volume, 63% margin/deleveraging.** Not as extreme as TOA (almost 100% margin), but the base year was a cost-spike trough (GM 28.7%, CFO negative), which is exactly what the "~26%" CAGR is measuring.

**Where 2026 growth is coming from (H1/26 NP +25% on sales +4%):**
- Company's own words: "lower raw material costs, improved production efficiency, and effective management of production costs and operating expenses" (CN 14 Aug 2026); Q1: "average raw material cost slightly lower than the prior year" (SN 3M/2026).
- Dairy-fat cycle: EU butter record €8,375/t Nov 2024 → €6,700/t Aug 2025 → €4,090/t Dec 2025 → €3,835/t Feb 2026 (lowest since 2021), -35% y-y Apr 2026 (dairynews.today, vespertool.com, expana Q2/2026 report). GDT butter 4,740 USD/t on 6 Oct 2026, -20 USD on the event, i.e. flat (commodity-board.com). KCG's butter is made from imported anhydrous milk fat (AMF) and it imports cheese — the company said in Aug 2024 it had pre-purchased AMF supply (panphol Q2/2568, unreliable source). Numeric cost data per input is not disclosed.
- `guess:` KCG is at the sweet point of the cycle: input bought at 5-year lows, selling prices unchanged ("no annual price increase policy; focus on efficiency" — panphol Q2/2568). If dairy fat turns up in 2027, GM reverts toward 29–30% and NP growth stops, as in 2022.
- Structural helper that is permanent but one-time: Thailand's import tariff on butter and cheese from Australia/New Zealand fell to zero on 1 Jan 2025 after a 20-year phase-down (MFAT NZ, dairynews.today Jan 2025). `guess:` this lowered KCG's AMF/cheese cost in 2025–26 and also lowers the barrier for competing imports — cuts both ways; the company has not quantified it.
- Sales side: Q1/26 all channels and all product groups grew, biscuits returned to growth after a 2025 decline (SN 3M/2026); Q2/26 sales +0.3% with "overall market subdued" (CN); management: "sales have passed through their most challenging period of slowdown in Q2/2026 … slight growth in Q3 and Q4" (CN 14 Aug 2026). Export is 4–6% of revenue — too small to move the total for years.
- Capacity: butter +25% completed Dec 2025 (SN FY2025 — company source). Cheese +10% and biscuit +20% "by Q4/2569" appear only in panphol summaries (not verified by company documents). 2026 capex plan: automation, heat recovery, solar (SN FY2025); Krungsri estimated capex 300–400 m (hoonvision 15 Dec 2025).

**Is ~26%/yr sustainable for 3 more years?** No, by the company's own plan (NP +10–15%/yr, NP 2028 750–800 m = 14–17%/yr from 2025). To hit 26% for three more years NP 2028 would need to be ~1,000 m, i.e. NM 10% on 10 bn sales — KCG has never exceeded 7.0% in a quarter. `guess:` probability <10%. What is plausible: 10–17%/yr from sales +5–8% + NM creeping toward 7% via SG&A leverage and automation — a Hong "growth" stock at the lower bound, with a 5–6% yield.

**Why the scan shows CAGR 27.8% while the keep-or-cut table shows 17%:** both are correct for different questions.
- The scanner's `earnings_cagr_3y()` compounds **net income** 2022→2025 from the cache (`hong_stage1_scanner.py:416-433`): (503.3/241.1)^(1/3) - 1 = 27.8%.
- The 17% figure compounds SET's **reported EPS** 0.67 (2023) → 0.92 (2025). But 2023 EPS 0.67 is on the weighted-average share count for a year in which 155 m new shares were issued in August (IPO); NP 305.9 on the full 545 m shares is 0.56 (Trinity's "Norm EPS 0.56", TR p.4). 2022 EPS 0.62 is on ~390 m pre-IPO shares. So "EPS CAGR" mixes share counts and understates growth for anyone buying at today's share count.
