## 1. One-page answer

**Verdict: growth is real in the accounts but not of the kind Hong buys — do not enter at the full 7.5% slot; at most a small position (≤ 3.75%) and only after Q3/26 (due ~13 Nov 2026) confirms the H1 jump was not an expense-accounting effect.** Confidence: **medium-high** on the numbers (company filings read directly), **medium** on the verdict (one unexplained line decides it).

Why:
1. **The 3-year "+100%/yr EPS CAGR" is a base effect.** Net profit 2022 was 17.3 M (NM 1.0%) after 146.2 M in 2021 and 185.7 M in 2018. 2025 = 161.0 M is still **below 2018**; only TTM (201.1 M) is a new high, and on today's 498.7 M shares (+52% vs 2021 from warrant exercise + stock dividend) EPS TTM 0.40 is only just above 2018's 0.37 (S · C · SA · 56-1). Revenue 2021→2025 **fell** (1,826 → 1,736 M). All of the profit growth came from gross margin (33.4% → 46.9%) and from exiting loss-making food products — not from selling more.
2. **H1/26 (+63%) is built on a selling-expense cut, not on margin.** Sales +5.4%, but COGS rose from 54.1% to 58.5% of sales (GM −4.4 pts); selling expenses fell 48.5% (−94.3 M) "mainly driven by accounting adjustments alongside … realignment of advertising plans"; admin +36% (+29.8 M, headcount + higher doubtful-debt allowance) (MDA-H1). The net +40 M of profit is therefore ≈ the expense "adjustment". The company does not say what the adjustment is. Q1/26 alone was +1.4%; Q2/26 +111% (F45).
3. **Cash conversion broke in H1/26**: CFO 30.2 M vs NP 103.7 M (0.29×); Q2 alone 3.9 M; working-capital build −124 M, receivables 184 → 226 M (S · SA). FY2025 was fine (1.67×). One half-year is not a verdict, but Hong criterion 4 fails on the latest data.
4. **What is genuinely good**: net cash 358 M (cash 452 M vs interest-bearing + lease + related-party debt 94 M, Jun-26, SA); ROE 20.9% (2025) / ~25% TTM; yield 6.7% on FY2025 DPS 0.325; PE TTM 12.1; GM 47% is high for a household-chemical maker; management did exit the losing F&B lines (F&B revenue 162 → 97 M, Lottofood closed 2024) (56-1).
5. **Structural problems that do not go away with a good Q3**: (a) liquidity — 3-month average 16,700 shares ≈ **80 k baht/day**, median 5,000 shares, 2 zero-volume days (Y); a 7.5% slot (~235 k baht at the portfolio size implied by the TOA deep dive) is three days of the entire market's turnover; (b) float — 77.7 M shares = 15.6% (SA), right at SET's 15% minimum (SET's official figure not retrieved); (c) control — TOA Venture Holding 66.13% (same Tangkaravakoon family as TOA, already the largest Hong-leg name), acting CEO is a family member, related-party loans run both ways (56-1 · PP); (d) payout 100.7% of 2025 EPS (56-1) — the dividend cannot grow faster than EPS and is cut the moment EPS dips.

**What Art must decide (not an order):**
- A. Whether a stock whose "growth" is margin repair + expense cuts, with flat sales, qualifies as a Hong growth name at all — the same question already open for TOA (KoC §TOA). SWC is a smaller, less liquid TOA-family version of the same pattern.
- B. If entering: size. Section 9 argues 3.75% is the ceiling because of turnover, not because of valuation.
- C. Whether to wait for Q3/26 (~13 Nov 2026) — the single data point that resolves point 2 above. Price is already +25% over 52 weeks and +71% from end-2024, so the market has partly moved.
- D. Concentration in one family: TOA (7.5% slot) + SWC would put two Tangkaravakoon-controlled companies in a 25% leg.

## 4. Growth story

**Where the 2022→2025 profit growth came from (NP +143.7 M):**
- Sales +42 M (1,694 → 1,736, +2.5% in three years) → at 2022's 1.0% NM, worth ~0.4 M.
- Gross margin 33.4% → 46.9% (+13.5 pts × 1,736 M) = **+234 M gross profit** — the whole story. Drivers per company: mix shift to high-margin products, production-cost control (MDA-FY25), and in 2025 "changed the F&B distribution model and discontinued loss-making products" (MDA-9M25). `guess:` raw-material prices falling from 2022 peaks also helped (company says raw materials = 83.7% of COGS; it does not quantify input prices).
- Offsets: selling expenses 2025 +13% (410 M), admin +16% (176 M) (MDA-FY25).
- **Is 2022 an abnormal base? Yes.** 2022 NP 17.3 M vs 146.2 M (2021), 185.7 M (2018): Q3/22 was a loss (−11.0 M), GM collapsed to 33%, F&B losses. The 3-year CAGR measures recovery from a trough, not expansion.

**Where H1/26 (+63%) came from (NP +40.1 M):**
- Sales +43.6 M (+5.4%) → at 2025 NM, worth ~4 M.
- GM −4.4 pts × 844 M = **−37 M**.
- Selling expenses **−94.3 M** ("accounting adjustments" + marketing realignment).
- Admin **+29.8 M** (headcount, doubtful-debt allowance).
- Net ≈ +40 M. Strip out the selling-expense line and H1/26 profit would have been *down* ~50 M y-y. The sustainability of H1/26 depends entirely on whether the 94 M is a permanent cost reset (marketing spend 2025 had risen 33% in 9M — MDA-9M25 — so some reversal is plausible) or a one-time accrual reversal. The MD&A does not say. **This is the single question for Q3/26.**

**Is it sustainable?**
- Sales engine: household chemicals (72%) grow with retail distribution (traditional trade 45%); company's own full-year 2025 sales growth was +3.0%; 9M/25 was −1.0% (MDA-9M25). Export 10.9%, 19 countries (OPP22), Australia subsidiary 50%. No evidence of a sales engine above mid-single digits. New legs (The Goody, biostimulants) are tiny today.
- Margin engine: GM 47–48% in Q2–Q4/25 is the highest in the data (2015–2021 GM 34–41%, OPP22 slide 27 COGS 57.9–63.6%); H1/26 already gave back 4 pts. More upside from mix is possible but the easy part (dropping losing lines) is done.
- `guess:` probability that NP grows ≥ 26%/yr through 2028 from the 2025 base: < 15%. Needs both GM back to 47%+ and sales +10%/yr, neither seen since 2018.

**Three profit scenarios (our assumptions, not forecasts; NP attributable, M baht; EPS on 498.7 M shares):**

| Scenario | Assumptions | NP 2026 / EPS | NP 2028 / EPS | 2025→28 CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bad (35%) | Selling-expense "adjustment" reverses in H2; GM stays ~41%; sales +3%. H2/26 NP ~75 M (vs 97 M H2/25) | 178 / 0.36 (+11%) | 165 / 0.33 | +1% |
| Base (45%) | H2/26 +15% y-y (cost reset partly real, GM 42–43%); then +8%/yr on sales +4% and flat margin | 215 / 0.43 (+34%) | 250 / 0.50 | +16% |
| Good (20%) | GM back to 45%, cost base stays low, The Goody/biostimulants add 100 M sales by 2028 | 240 / 0.48 (+49%) | 325 / 0.65 | +26% |

Only the good case reaches Hong's 26%; it requires every H1/26 gain to be permanent plus a new product line succeeding — the company's track record on new lines (F&B) argues against.
