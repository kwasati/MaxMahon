## 1. One-page answer

**Verdict as candidate: do not enter as the Hong "growth" slot now. If Art wants exposure anyway, treat it as a small cyclical/dividend position (≤ 3.75% of portfolio) and only after Q3/26 (≈ 12 Nov 2026) and the SEC outcome on the deputy CEO are known.** Confidence: **high** that the 2026 jump is price-driven, not volume-driven (company says so itself); **medium** on the 2027 numbers in §4; **medium** on how the governance case plays out.

Why:
1. **The 2026 jump is a base-oil price windfall, in the company's own words.** Q2/26 sales +19.4% y-y "mainly due to the increase in the average selling price in line with the increase in global base oil prices, following the reduction in base oil supply ... directly affected by the conflict in the Middle East and the opening and closure of transportation routes through the Strait of Hormuz" (MDA p.6). Gross margin 20.70% in Q2/26 vs 13.69% a year earlier and 11.1–13.9% every full year since 2023 (MDA; FS). The margin gain is attributed to "higher average selling price, efficient inventory management, and price negotiations with suppliers" (MDA p.8) — i.e. selling inventory bought at pre-spike cost into a spiked price. H1/26 sales volume is not disclosed; Q1/26 sales were −6.8% y-y with "slower customer purchase orders" (FS; psp.co.th PR 13 May 2026), so volume growth is not the story.
2. **Underlying growth record is short and lumpy, not 26%/yr for 3 years.** Net profit 2023 −20% / 2024 +57% / 2025 +27% / H1-26 +53% (SS; FS). 2-y EPS CAGR 2023→25 = 26.7% (0.38→0.61) but 3-y net-profit CAGR 2022→25 = 16.5% and 3-y EPS CAGR ≈ 6% because 2022 had 1,050 M shares vs 1,400 M after IPO (C; SS). The structural market grows ~2–2.5%/yr (Kline via MDA p.3). Management's own JUMP+ plan (Apr 2026) targets 1,000 M net profit **by 2028** — H1/26 alone is 801 M, so either the target was far too conservative or management did not expect the windfall (hoonvision 10 Apr 2026; hoonsmart 8 Sep 2026 "2026 > 1,000 M").
3. **Governance flag, fresh.** On 1 Sep 2026 the SEC imposed civil sanctions on 18 persons for manipulating META shares in 2018–2020; one is **Mr Sakesan Krongphanich — PSP director, Deputy CEO/COO, largest shareholder 13.31%, and the signatory of the Q2/26 MD&A**: fine 2,949,746 baht, 11-month trading ban, **22-month ban from being a director/executive of a listed company** if he accepts (SEC via thaipr 3756237; isranews 148722). PSP's 2 Sep 2026 clarification: personal matter, pre-IPO, "no impact on operations", he is "consulting with the SEC" (share2trade 58873). As of 28 Oct 2026 he is still listed as director (settrade board page). He is the public face of the M&A/JUMP+ strategy.
4. **Balance sheet moved the wrong way for a Hong stock in H1/26.** Total liabilities/equity 0.82 → 1.17, interest-bearing debt/equity 0.38 → 0.54, goodwill +559 M from buying TTU Holding (tyre importer, 214 "Safe Tyre" shops) and WhatsEGG (parts platform); CFO H1/26 = 178 M vs net profit 801 M because receivables +880 M and inventories +827 M as prices rose (MDA p.9–10; SS). Cash ≈ 441 M vs short-term bank borrowings ≈ 1.8 bn → Hong criterion 2 (cash covers short debt + dividend) fails, as it already did in May (S2: 0.30x).
5. **What is genuinely good:** ROE 21.7% (2025) / 25.9% annualised (H1/26); profit at all-time high two years running; PE 9.4 on TTM EPS 0.80; dividend raised every year since listing (0.15 → 0.20 → 0.28; interim FY26 already 0.30); PwC unqualified; CG 5 stars, SET ESG "A" (FS; SS; hoonsmart 8 Sep 2026). As a **cheap cyclical with a rising payout** it is defensible; as a **26%-compounder** it is not.

**What Art must decide (not orders):**
- a. Whether the Hong leg should hold a commodity-windfall stock at all. Hong's criterion 7 wants earnings compounding from the business, not from base-oil prices.
- b. Whether the 22-month executive ban (if accepted) on the largest shareholder/deputy CEO is acceptable under "honest management". The company has not said what the board will do.
- c. If entering anyway: size (my suggestion ≤ 3.75%, §9) and timing — Q3/26 (≈ 12 Nov) will show whether GM stays near 20% or falls back toward 14%.
- d. A pre-agreed rule for 2027: if Q2/27 profit is down y-y against the Q2/26 windfall base, is that a "cut" trigger or an expected normalisation? Decide before the numbers arrive.

## 4. The growth story

**Where the H1/26 profit increase (+276 M) comes from (MDA; SS):**
- Sales revenue +377 M (+5.7%) → at 2025 GM 13.9% that is only ≈ +52 M of gross profit.
- Gross margin 14.13% → 18.17% (+4.0 pts) on 7,280 M revenue = **≈ +294 M** → essentially the whole increase is margin, and the company attributes margin to average selling price following base-oil prices plus inventory management.
- Q2/26 alone: sales +626 M (+19.4%), gross profit +370 M (+91%), GM 20.7%. Part of the Q2 revenue step is TTU consolidated from ~mid-May (tyres, low margin) — size not disclosed (§10). Lower selling expenses and finance costs helped; higher admin costs and lower associates' profit hurt (MDA p.7).
- Volume: not disclosed. Q1/26 sales −6.8% with "slower customer purchase orders ... both lubricant and base oil" and "regional export challenges from border unrest" (psp.co.th PR 13 May 2026). So H1 volume is `guess:` flat to down.
- Counter-evidence that it is not purely one-off: GM rose 11.1 → 12.7 → 13.9% in 2023–25 before the spike (mix toward specialty products, procurement, 2025 recycling) — a real but slow +1 pt/yr trend.

**Is it sustainable?**
- Asia base-oil prices were still rising in late Sep–Oct 2026 (Group II 500N $910–950/t FOB Asia, +$10/t late Sep; "tight supply and demand ... high crude and feedstock prices" through Oct; lubesngreases Asia reports) while Brent eased from $96.96 (28 Sep) to $89.57 (26 Oct). So Q3/26 ASP likely stays elevated, but the inventory gain shrinks as replacement cost catches up; when prices turn down the same mechanism reverses (inventory loss, as in 2023 when GM fell to 11.1% and profit −20%).
- Market growth: Thailand lubricants ~2.0%/yr, Asia ~2.4% (Kline via MDA). BEV 491k = ~1% of vehicles; hybrids 61.5% of EVs still need lubricants (MDA p.4–5).
- Growth engines beyond the cycle: export (22.9% → 30%), RRBO revenue from 2027, TTU/WhatsEGG. None is sized by the company; Yuanta sized TTU at 15–30 M/yr (~2–3% of profit).
- **What would have to happen for ~26%/yr for 3 more years:** base-oil prices staying at 2026 levels **and** rising further each year, or M&A at a scale far beyond anything announced. `guess:` probability well under 20%.
